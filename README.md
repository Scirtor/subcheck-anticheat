## Findings and business idea updates

The study uses **20 survey participants and 3 recorded interviews**. Seventeen of nineteen rate cheating seriousness at 4–5, and sixteen of twenty rate the described server-side concept’s usefulness at 4–5. Detection accuracy, false positives and telemetry privacy remain the main adoption concerns. Six of nine technical-experience respondents select self-hosting.

SUB-CHECK remains a proposed B2B tool for studios operating competitive shooters. It would add server-side aim analysis beside existing anti-cheat, return supporting events with a risk score and help moderators review cases. The updated proposal adds deployment choice, minimal telemetry, a review/appeal trail, a bounded free pilot and qualification of the studio’s ability to export the needed data. The interviews distinguish aim-cheat review from adjacent mobile-economy and leaderboard-protection needs. They also identify the need for asynchronous processing that cannot punish players for missing telemetry or service failures.

Concept interest is not a purchase commitment or proof of detection effectiveness. Buyer demand, paid conversion, integration cost and technical quality require a pilot.
