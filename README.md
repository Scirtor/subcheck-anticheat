The deck has 12 scenes. Right arrow or Space shows the next point, then the next scene. Left arrow returns to the previous scene. R restarts the scene. N opens speaker hints. F opens full screen. ## Main findings

We used **20 survey participants and 3 recorded interviews**. Seventeen of nineteen rated the cheating problem at 4–5 out of 5. Sixteen of twenty rated the idea’s usefulness at 4–5. Accuracy, wrong alerts and game data privacy are important concerns. Six of nine technical participants selected running the service on their own servers.

SUB-CHECK remains a proposed B2B service for small competitive shooter studios. It would study aiming with game server data and give moderators event evidence and a risk score. It would work beside current anti-cheat. People would make permanent-ban decisions.

The updated plan adds data control, local or cloud server options, a review and appeal record, and a limited free pilot. Before a pilot, we should confirm that a studio has an aiming problem and can share the needed data. Analysis must not stop gameplay. Missing data or analysis failures alone must not punish a player.

Interest in the idea does not prove product quality or payment. We still need studio buyers, practical tests and specific paid offers. The targets of 8 buyer interviews, 3 studio pilots and 20% less median review time are future test goals, not achieved results.
