## 1. Executive Summary

This report outlines the Customer Development (CustDev) study for **SUB-CHECK**, a server-side behavioral anti-cheat platform. The goal of this research is to validate customer jobs, pains, and gains within the competitive gaming market—specifically among competitive players, community organizers, and independent game studios—and test market viability for an OS-agnostic, server-side telemetry approach.

## 3. Customer Discovery Methodology

Our interview framework adheres to *The Mom Test* principles:
- Focus on past behavior rather than hypothetical opinions.
- Avoid pitching the solution during discovery questions.
- Probe into emotional and financial costs of dealing with cheating and anti-cheat software.

### Primary Hypotheses to Test:
1. **Kernel Driver Friction:** Players experience technical instability (crashes, BSOD) and privacy concerns with Ring 0 drivers.
2. **Hardware Exploit Gap:** Competitive organizers face growing evasion by hardware cheats (DMA cards, mouse macros) that client anti-cheats cannot reliably detect.
3. **Platform Incompatibility:** Linux and handheld (Steam Deck) gamers are disproportionately locked out by modern client anti-cheats.
4. **Indie Developer Barrier:** Indie multiplayer developers lack affordable, lightweight anti-cheat solutions.
