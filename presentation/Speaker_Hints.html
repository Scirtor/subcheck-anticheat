01 SUB-CHECK

Time: 35 seconds.

Say: Hello. We are presenting SUB-CHECK, a proposed anti-cheat service for small shooter studios. It would analyse aiming behaviour using game server data. Moderators would receive a risk score and event evidence. Our study explores the problem and reactions to this idea. We have not yet proved product quality or customer payment.

Hint: Introduce the team. Say “proposed service” clearly. Do not describe it as a finished product.

Transition: First, we explain our research material.

Source: Technology Entrepreneurship. Assignment 3. 8 October 2026.

02 Research material

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: We used twenty survey participants and three recorded interviews. Some questions have nineteen answers. The technical section has nine. We received summary charts, so we cannot connect answers to individual people. We analyse interviews separately because we do not know if participants overlap. The available material remains below the course minimum.

Hint: Say the actual counts first. Explain the missing minimum once, without adding the two groups.

Transition: Next, we separate buyers, users and players.

Source: Sources: 27 survey screenshots and three recordings. Course instructions.

03 Customer roles

Time: 40 seconds.

Say: Our buyer is a lead at a small or medium-sized shooter studio. The buyer controls the budget and the decision to connect the service. Moderators are the daily users. Players benefit from fair matches and careful decisions. The survey includes only two people who selected game developer. Our next interviews should focus on actual studio buyers.

Hint: Explain that buyer and user can be different people. Do not call all nine technical participants buyers.

Transition: The survey gives an early view of the cheating problem.

Source: Sources: Assignment 2, Q01 and Q03. Future recruitment is our plan.

04 The cheating problem

Time: 40 seconds.

Say: Seventeen of nineteen participants rated the cheating problem at four or five out of five. This is about ninety percent. Fourteen of twenty met cheating often or sometimes. Satisfaction with current tools averaged two point nine five out of five. These answers show a relevant problem in our sample. They do not describe every player or studio.

Hint: Point to the counts before the percentages. “17 out of 19” is easier to say than 89.5%.

Transition: We then asked about the SUB-CHECK idea.

Source: Sources: Q05 n=19, Q06 n=20, Q10 n=19.

05 Interest and concerns

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: Sixteen of twenty rated the idea’s usefulness at four or five. However, eleven selected accuracy as a concern. Nine selected wrong alerts, and nine selected game data privacy. Seven selected server costs. People could choose several concerns. These results help us choose what to test first. They do not prove that the service detects cheating well.

Hint: Reveal the bars one at a time. A wrong alert means a fair player receives a cheating alert.

Transition: This leads to the question of fair review.

Source: Sources: Q13 and Q16, n=20. People could select several concerns.

06 Review before permanent bans

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: Twelve participants selected manual review, and twelve selected extra checks. These groups may overlap. All twenty said yes or probably yes when asked whether review before a permanent ban would increase trust. The question describes review positively. We still need to test whether people accept its time and cost. Our pilot keeps permanent-ban decisions with moderators.

Hint: Do not add the two groups of twelve. Say that trust is a stated opinion, not measured behaviour.

Transition: The technical answers also show a need for control.

Source: Sources: Q17 and Q18, n=20. Several actions could be selected.

07 Where the service should run

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: Six of nine technical participants selected running the service on their own servers. Three selected managed cloud. An API, SDK and engine plugin each received four selections. These options can overlap. Five would share data only with strict privacy controls. We should compare server options in a pilot. This small group cannot decide the whole product design.

Hint: Own servers means self-hosting. API connects systems. SDK gives code tools. Define terms only if needed.

Transition: The interviews show why these choices matter.

Source: Sources: Q21 and Q23, n=9. These are technical participants, not confirmed buyers.

08 Interview findings

Time: 60 seconds.

Say: The mobile game director mainly needs protection for game values, such as money. This is a limited fit for our first aiming product. The independent developer checked logs after a score filter wrongly rejected users. The third developer said data analysis must not stop gameplay and can still add extra load. These interviews support careful review and reliable integration. None includes a purchase commitment.

Hint: Describe one example per interview. Use anonymous IDs. Do not present their needs as one common vote.

Transition: We used these findings to update the idea.

Source: Sources: I01 03:32–04:12, I02 01:48–02:20, I03 12:00–13:17 and 15:15–17:17.

09 Changes to the idea

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: Assignment two already focused on shooter studios and human review. We keep these choices. We now add clearer event evidence, saved review decisions and data controls. We also compare local and cloud deployment. Before a pilot, we confirm that the studio has an aiming problem and can share the needed data. Missing data alone must never cause punishment.

Hint: Separate earlier choices from new changes. Mention that gameplay continues during analysis failures.

Transition: The updated business model follows these choices.

Source: Sources: Assignment 2, survey Q16–Q25 and I01–I03.

10 Updated business model

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: The business model has nine parts. Shooter studios are our customers. The proposed value is useful aim evidence and fair review. We plan to reach studios through developer contacts and guides. We would help with integration and support. Revenue may come from monthly or usage prices after a free pilot. Our main costs include development, servers and support. These are working hypotheses.

Hint: Do not read every box. Explain customer, value, revenue and costs. No price has been confirmed.

Transition: We need practical tests before stronger business claims.

Source: Working hypotheses. No confirmed prices or measured service costs.

11 Next tests

Time: 45 seconds.

Say: We propose at least eight interviews with studio leads or moderators. We would continue if three studios offer concrete pilots with available data and a responsible person. We would test correct alerts and wrong alerts using separate test players. We also target twenty percent less median review time, with no worse decisions. These are future targets. We have not achieved them.

Hint: Stress “propose” and “target”. The 20% is a review-time goal, not detection accuracy.

Transition: Finally, we state what the study can support.

Source: Proposed targets. We have not achieved these results.

12 Conclusion

Time: 30 seconds.

Say: Our study supports a focused pilot for SUB-CHECK. The main priorities are detection quality, fewer wrong alerts and control over game data. We still need to test studio demand, actual payment and technical performance. We also need more participant evidence to meet the course minimum. Thank you. We are ready for questions.

Hint: End with what you learned and what you will test. Avoid saying the product or business model is fully validated.

Transition: Questions and discussion.

Source: Sources: survey summaries, interview notes and course requirements.

Short answers for questions

Why only 20 survey participants? We analysed all the material available to us. The sample is below the course minimum. We need more participant records.

Did interviews prove that studios will buy? No. They show problems and conditions. We have no paid commitment.

Can the system detect all hardware cheats? We have not tested this. We need labelled game data and a game-specific test.

Why use human review? A score can be wrong. Event evidence and review can help make fair decisions. We still need to test review time and quality.

Is the software open source? We have not chosen a licence. The survey suggests interest in a free or open-source core.

What is telemetry? It is game data sent for analysis, such as aim events. We should collect only what the test needs.

Useful number phrases

17/19: seventeen out of nineteen. 16/20: sixteen out of twenty. 2.95: two point nine five. 20% target: a target of twenty percent less median review time. Median: the middle value when times are in order.

Words to explain

False positive: a wrong alert about a fair player. Self-hosting: running the service on the studio’s own servers. Risk score: a signal for review priority. It is not proof. Pilot: a small practical test.
